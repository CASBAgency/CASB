What Documents Do You Need for an Insurance Claim? A Complete Checklist for Malaysians

By CASB Advisory Team · July 22, 2026 · 25 min read

Introduction

When making an insurance claim, having the right documents is just as important as completing the claim form.

An insurer cannot properly assess a claim based only on a receipt or a brief explanation of what happened. It may need evidence confirming:

  • Who is making the claim
  • Which policy the claim relates to
  • What medical condition or event occurred
  • When it happened
  • What treatment was received
  • How much was paid
  • Who is legally entitled to receive the payment

Missing, incomplete or inconsistent documents are among the most common reasons an insurance claim takes longer than expected.

This guide explains the documents commonly required for medical, hospitalisation, critical illness, accident, disability and death claims in Malaysia.

The exact requirements will differ according to the insurer, policy, benefit and individual circumstances. Always refer to the insurer’s official claim checklist before submitting your documents.

Why Does an Insurer Need So Many Documents?

Insurance claims must be assessed according to the policy contract.

Before paying a claim, the insurer generally needs to confirm that:

  • The policy was active when the event occurred
  • The insured person is covered
  • The event falls within the policy benefits
  • No waiting period or exclusion applies
  • The treatment or diagnosis meets the policy definition
  • The claimant is legally entitled to receive the benefit
  • The amount claimed is supported by proper evidence

For medical claims, the insurer may also need to determine whether the treatment was medically necessary, related to a pre-existing condition or connected to earlier symptoms.

The insurer may therefore require information from several different parties, including:

  • The policyholder
  • The insured person
  • The claimant
  • The attending doctor
  • The hospital
  • Government departments
  • The police
  • The employer
  • Other insurers

Documents Commonly Required for Most Insurance Claims

Although each claim type has different requirements, several documents are commonly requested across most claims.

1. Completed Claim Form

The claim form provides the insurer with the basic information needed to begin assessment.

It may ask for:

  • Policy number
  • Name of the insured person
  • Claimant’s details
  • Contact information
  • Date of diagnosis, treatment, accident or death
  • Nature of the claim
  • Medical history
  • Bank account information
  • Declaration and consent

The form should be completed accurately and signed by the correct person.

Allianz Malaysia’s life claims process, for example, states that the claimant should complete the Claimant’s Statement, while the treating doctor should complete the relevant Medical Report. Supporting documents listed in the applicable checklist should then be submitted with the claim.

2. Copy of NRIC or Passport

The insurer may require identification documents for:

  • The insured person
  • The policyholder
  • The claimant
  • The nominee
  • The beneficiary
  • The legal representative

Copies should normally be clear and show both sides of the NRIC where applicable.

For foreign nationals, the insurer may request:

  • Passport identification page
  • Visa or permit
  • Pages showing entry and exit dates
  • Proof of residence

3. Policy or Certificate Information

You may be asked to provide:

  • Policy number
  • Certificate number
  • Copy of the policy schedule
  • Medical card details
  • Employer group policy information
  • Membership number

The insurer may already have access to this information, but including the correct policy number helps prevent the claim from being linked to the wrong policy.

4. Bank Account Details

Approved claim payments are commonly made by direct credit.

You may need to submit:

  • E-payment or direct-credit authorisation form
  • Bank account number
  • Name of the bank
  • Copy of bank statement
  • Copy of bank passbook
  • Screenshot showing the account holder’s name and account number

The account name should usually match the person legally entitled to receive the claim payment.

Allianz Malaysia lists a Claims Notification Form and an E-Payment Form among the documents applicable to claims.

5. Medical Authorisation or Consent

The insurer may require written consent to:

  • Obtain medical records
  • Contact hospitals and clinics
  • Speak to treating doctors
  • Review previous medical history
  • Obtain investigation results
  • Verify information with other insurers

This consent allows the insurer to collect information necessary for the claim assessment.

Documents for a Medical or Hospitalisation Claim

The documents required depend on whether the claim is handled through a cashless Guarantee Letter or reimbursement.

With a cashless claim, the hospital usually communicates directly with the insurer.

With a reimbursement claim, the policyholder generally needs to submit the supporting documents after paying the medical expenses.

Medical Claim Document Checklist

Common documents include:

  • Completed medical or hospitalisation claim form
  • Claimant’s statement
  • Medical report completed by the treating doctor
  • Original itemised hospital bill
  • Original official receipt
  • Final tax invoice
  • Discharge summary
  • Admission and discharge records
  • Referral letter
  • Diagnostic reports
  • Investigation results
  • Prescription or medication list
  • Proof of payment
  • NRIC or passport copy
  • E-payment form
  • Bank account details

Allianz Malaysia’s reimbursement guidance lists documents such as the completed claim form, medical report, original itemised bills and receipts, original medical invoice, police report where applicable, passport entry and exit pages for overseas treatment and a completed e-payment form.

What Is an Itemised Hospital Bill?

An itemised bill is a detailed breakdown of the hospital charges.

It may list:

  • Room and board
  • Doctor’s fees
  • Surgical fees
  • Anaesthetist’s fees
  • Operating theatre charges
  • Medication
  • Laboratory tests
  • Imaging
  • Medical equipment
  • Nursing care
  • Administrative charges
  • Other hospital services

A credit-card receipt or payment slip by itself is usually not enough because it only proves that a payment was made. It does not show what the payment was for.

Allianz’s corporate hospitalisation claim checklist, for example, asks for detailed bills showing doctor’s fees, medication charges and investigation charges, along with relevant diagnostic reports.

What Is an Official Receipt?

An official receipt confirms that the hospital bill has been paid.

It should usually show:

  • Patient’s name
  • Receipt number
  • Date of payment
  • Amount paid
  • Payment method
  • Hospital or clinic details

A hospital bill marked “outstanding” or “amount due” does not prove that payment was made.

For reimbursement claims, both the bill and proof of payment may be required.

What Is a Discharge Summary?

A discharge summary is prepared when the patient leaves the hospital.

It commonly records:

  • Reason for admission
  • Symptoms
  • Diagnosis
  • Treatment provided
  • Procedure performed
  • Medication given
  • Patient’s condition upon discharge
  • Follow-up plan

The discharge summary helps the insurer understand what occurred during the admission.

However, it may not replace a full medical report where the insurer requires more detailed information.

What Is a Medical Report?

A medical report is usually completed by the doctor who treated or diagnosed the patient.

It may include:

  • Patient’s symptoms
  • Date symptoms began
  • First consultation date
  • Diagnosis
  • Relevant medical history
  • Investigation findings
  • Treatment provided
  • Procedure performed
  • Cause of the condition
  • Whether the condition is related to an accident
  • Whether similar symptoms existed previously
  • Doctor’s professional opinion

The claimant should not complete the doctor’s section personally.

Allianz provides different medical report forms for hospitalisation, accident, disability and various critical illnesses.

Common Diagnostic Reports Required

Depending on the condition, the insurer may request:

  • Blood test report
  • Urine test report
  • X-ray report
  • Ultrasound report
  • CT scan report
  • MRI report
  • Endoscopy report
  • Angiogram report
  • Electrocardiogram report
  • Echocardiogram report
  • Histopathology report
  • Biopsy report
  • Laboratory culture report
  • Surgical report
  • Anaesthetic report

Diagnostic reports help confirm the diagnosis rather than relying only on the name of the condition written on a bill.

Documents for Pre-Hospitalisation and Post-Hospitalisation Claims

Some medical policies cover eligible treatment received before or after hospital admission.

Documents may include:

  • Specialist referral letter
  • Consultation bills
  • Original receipts
  • Diagnostic test reports
  • Prescription
  • Pharmacy receipt
  • Follow-up appointment record
  • Physiotherapy records
  • Proof that the treatment relates to the hospital admission

The insurer may need to confirm that the outpatient expenses are directly related to the covered hospitalisation and occurred within the benefit period stated in the policy.

Documents for Outpatient Accident Claims

For accidental outpatient treatment, you may need:

  • Accident claim form
  • Medical report
  • Clinic or hospital bills
  • Original receipts
  • Doctor’s notes
  • X-ray or scan reports
  • Police report, where applicable
  • Accident description
  • Photographs
  • Employer accident report
  • Proof of the date and location of the accident

The accident description should be clear and consistent.

Include:

  • What happened
  • When it happened
  • Where it happened
  • What injury occurred
  • When treatment was first obtained

Documents for a Critical Illness Claim

Critical illness insurance usually pays a lump-sum benefit when the insured person is diagnosed with a covered condition that meets the definition in the policy.

It is different from a medical-card claim.

The amount paid is generally based on the insured benefit, not the hospital bill.

Critical Illness Claim Checklist

Commonly required documents include:

  • Completed critical illness claim form
  • Claimant’s statement
  • Medical report completed by the specialist
  • NRIC or passport copy
  • Policy details
  • Diagnostic reports
  • Laboratory findings
  • Histopathology or biopsy report
  • Scan reports
  • Surgical report
  • Hospital discharge summary
  • Previous medical records
  • E-payment form
  • Bank account details

Different illnesses require different medical evidence.

For example, Allianz provides specialised medical report forms for heart-related conditions, cancer, kidney failure, brain and spinal conditions, as well as other covered critical illnesses.

Additional Documents for Cancer Claims

A cancer claim may require:

  • Histopathology report
  • Biopsy report
  • Imaging reports
  • Oncologist’s report
  • Staging information
  • Surgical report
  • Treatment plan
  • Hospital records
  • Laboratory results

A doctor’s note simply stating “cancer” may not be sufficient.

The insurer may need to confirm:

  • Type of cancer
  • Stage
  • Whether it is invasive
  • Tissue involved
  • Whether the diagnosis meets the policy definition

Additional Documents for Heart-Related Claims

A heart-related critical illness claim may require:

  • Cardiologist’s report
  • Electrocardiogram results
  • Cardiac enzyme test results
  • Coronary angiogram
  • Echocardiogram
  • Procedure report
  • Discharge summary
  • Surgical records

The insurer must assess whether the medical event satisfies the contractual definition of the covered heart condition.

Additional Documents for Stroke Claims

A stroke claim may require:

  • Neurologist’s report
  • CT or MRI report
  • Neurological examination findings
  • Rehabilitation records
  • Evidence of neurological deficit
  • Follow-up medical assessment
  • Discharge summary

Some policies require symptoms or functional impairment to persist for a specified period before the claim definition is satisfied.

Always check the policy wording.

Documents for a Total and Permanent Disability Claim

A Total and Permanent Disability claim generally requires evidence that the insured person meets the policy’s disability definition.

This may involve more than showing that the person is unable to work temporarily.

Disability Claim Checklist

Common documents include:

  • Completed disability claim form
  • Claimant’s statement
  • Medical report
  • Specialist’s assessment
  • Rehabilitation records
  • Diagnostic reports
  • Employment information
  • Job description
  • Proof of income
  • Medical leave records
  • Social Security Organisation records, where relevant
  • Employer’s confirmation
  • Evidence of inability to perform work or daily activities
  • NRIC or passport copy
  • Bank details

The insurer may request periodic medical updates if the disability assessment requires observation over time.

Documents for an Accident Claim

An accident claim may relate to:

  • Accidental medical expenses
  • Permanent disability
  • Temporary disability
  • Hospital income
  • Accidental death
  • Fracture benefit
  • Burns
  • Loss of limb
  • Other accident-related benefits

Accident Claim Checklist

Depending on the event, documents may include:

  • Completed accident claim form
  • Detailed accident statement
  • Police report
  • Medical report
  • Original bills and receipts
  • Investigation reports
  • X-rays or scans
  • Photographs of injuries
  • Photographs of the accident scene
  • Employer’s report
  • Witness information
  • Driving licence
  • Vehicle details
  • Occupational records
  • Proof of absence from work
  • Proof of income
  • NRIC or passport copy
  • Bank details

Allianz’s accident claim form states that eligible claim payments are credited to the bank account provided in the direct-credit authorisation form.

When Is a Police Report Required?

A police report may be required when the claim involves:

  • Road traffic accident
  • Assault
  • Suspicious injury
  • Accidental death
  • Criminal activity
  • Missing person
  • Workplace incident
  • Other circumstances requiring official investigation

The police report should accurately state what happened.

Do not exaggerate, guess or include details that are inconsistent with the medical report.

Differences between the police report, medical history and claim form may require further clarification.

Documents for a Death Claim

A life insurance death claim is usually submitted by:

  • Nominee
  • Beneficiary
  • Executor
  • Administrator
  • Trustee
  • Lawful next of kin
  • Other legally entitled person

The required documents depend on:

  • Whether there is a valid nomination
  • Cause of death
  • Place of death
  • Whether the death was accidental
  • Whether the death occurred overseas
  • Whether probate or letters of administration are required
  • Whether the policy is conventional or trust-based

Death Claim Checklist

Common documents include:

  • Completed death claim form
  • Claimant’s statement
  • Certified copy of death certificate
  • NRIC or passport of the deceased
  • NRIC or passport of the claimant
  • Proof of relationship
  • Policy details
  • Medical report
  • Cause-of-death documentation
  • Hospital records
  • Burial or cremation documents, where requested
  • E-payment form
  • Claimant’s bank details

Additional legal documents may be required where there is no valid nomination.

Additional Documents for Accidental Death

Where death results from an accident, the insurer may request:

  • Police report
  • Post-mortem report
  • Toxicology report
  • Coroner’s report
  • Inquest findings
  • Newspaper report
  • Accident photographs
  • Driving licence
  • Vehicle investigation documents
  • Witness statements
  • Investigation report from the relevant authority

The purpose is to establish the cause and circumstances of death.

Additional Documents for Death Overseas

For a death occurring outside Malaysia, documents may include:

  • Foreign death certificate
  • Certified translation
  • Passport showing travel dates
  • Embassy or consular confirmation
  • Medical report from the overseas hospital
  • Police report
  • Post-mortem report
  • Repatriation documents
  • Burial or cremation certificate
  • Proof of identity

The insurer may require foreign documents to be certified, legalised or translated by an approved translator.

What Happens When There Is No Nominee?

Where no valid nominee exists, the insurer may require legal documents showing who has authority to receive the policy proceeds.

These may include:

  • Grant of probate
  • Letters of administration
  • Distribution order
  • Small-estate distribution documents
  • Court order
  • Statutory declaration
  • Proof of relationship
  • Indemnity documents

The exact requirement depends on the policy, amount payable and applicable Malaysian law.

Having an up-to-date nomination can make the claim process easier for surviving family members.

Documents for a Hospital Income Benefit Claim

A hospital income benefit pays a fixed amount for each eligible day of hospitalisation.

It does not necessarily reimburse the actual hospital bill.

Commonly required documents include:

  • Claim form
  • Admission record
  • Discharge summary
  • Hospital bill showing admission and discharge dates
  • Medical report
  • Proof of diagnosis
  • Intensive care record, where relevant
  • NRIC or passport
  • Bank details

The insurer must verify the number of eligible hospitalisation days and whether the admission meets the policy conditions.

Documents for a Waiver of Premium Claim

A waiver benefit may pay or waive future premiums after a covered event such as:

  • Critical illness
  • Total and permanent disability
  • Death of the payor
  • Disability of the payor

Documents may include:

  • Waiver claim form
  • Medical report
  • Critical illness evidence
  • Disability assessment
  • Death certificate
  • Proof of relationship
  • Policy documents
  • Claimant’s identification
  • Supporting legal documents

The waiver usually applies only if the event meets the specific definition of the rider or benefit.

Documents for an Overseas Medical Claim

An overseas medical reimbursement claim may require:

  • Completed claim form
  • Original hospital bill
  • Original receipt
  • Medical report
  • Discharge summary
  • Diagnostic reports
  • Passport pages showing entry and exit
  • Travel itinerary
  • Flight details
  • Currency conversion evidence
  • Proof of payment
  • Certified English translation
  • Referral or emergency documentation

Allianz’s reimbursement checklist specifically notes that passport pages showing entry and exit dates may be required for overseas treatment.

Before planned overseas treatment, check whether:

  • Overseas treatment is covered
  • Prior approval is required
  • Coverage is limited to emergencies
  • Reimbursement is based on Malaysian treatment costs
  • Certain countries are excluded
  • Cashless overseas admission is available

Original Documents or Copies?

Whether originals are required depends on the insurer and submission method.

Original documents may commonly be requested for:

  • Hospital bills
  • Official receipts
  • Medical reports
  • Death certificates
  • Legal documents
  • Police reports

Digital or certified copies may be accepted in some situations.

Before submitting originals:

  1. Scan every page.
  2. Photograph important receipts.
  3. Keep a complete copy of the submission.
  4. Record the date submitted.
  5. Obtain an acknowledgement or reference number.
  6. Use trackable delivery if sending by post.

Do not write on original medical or payment documents unless instructed.

What Is a Certified True Copy?

A certified true copy is a copy confirmed by an authorised person as being a true reproduction of the original document.

Depending on the insurer, certification may be accepted from:

  • Commissioner for Oaths
  • Lawyer
  • Notary public
  • Insurer’s authorised officer
  • Government officer
  • Other approved person

Ask the insurer who is permitted to certify the document before arranging certification.

Who Should Complete the Claim Forms?

Different sections must be completed by different people.

The Claimant

The claimant usually completes:

  • Personal details
  • Claim description
  • Bank information
  • Declarations
  • Consent
  • Signature

The Treating Doctor

The doctor usually completes:

  • Diagnosis
  • Medical history
  • Date symptoms began
  • Investigation findings
  • Treatment details
  • Medical opinion
  • Cause of illness or injury

The Employer

The employer may need to confirm:

  • Occupation
  • Job duties
  • Employment status
  • Income
  • Absence from work
  • Group insurance membership
  • Workplace accident details

Do not complete or alter a section intended for a doctor, employer or official authority.

Common Document Mistakes That Delay Claims

Incomplete Claim Forms

Leaving questions unanswered may result in the form being returned.

Where a question does not apply, write “Not Applicable” if appropriate rather than leaving it blank.

Missing Signatures

Check every declaration and authorisation section.

A missing signature can prevent the insurer from obtaining medical records or processing payment.

Unclear Photocopies

Blurry, dark or cropped images may be rejected.

Ensure that:

  • All corners are visible
  • Text is readable
  • Identification numbers are clear
  • Both sides are included
  • No information is cut off

Submitting Only a Payment Slip

A credit-card slip proves that a payment occurred but does not explain the medical expenses.

Submit the itemised bill and official receipt.

Medical Report Completed by the Claimant

The treating doctor must complete the relevant medical section.

The claimant should not guess the diagnosis or alter medical information.

Different Dates Across Documents

The insurer may seek clarification when the claim form, medical report and hospital records show different dates for:

  • First symptoms
  • First consultation
  • Diagnosis
  • Accident
  • Admission
  • Treatment

Check the documents for obvious errors before submission.

Missing Diagnostic Evidence

A diagnosis may require objective evidence.

For example:

  • Cancer may require histopathology
  • Stroke may require CT or MRI evidence
  • Heart attack may require cardiac investigations
  • Fracture may require X-ray evidence

Submit the relevant reports when available.

Incorrect Bank Account Information

Check:

  • Account number
  • Bank name
  • Account holder’s name
  • Whether the account is active
  • Whether the claimant is entitled to receive payment

An incorrect account number can delay payment even after the claim is approved.

Failure to Submit Previous Medical Records

If the insurer requests earlier clinic or hospital records, the claim may remain pending until those records are received.

Previous medical information may be required to determine:

  • When symptoms began
  • Whether the condition is pre-existing
  • Whether the current diagnosis is related to earlier treatment
  • Whether medical information was disclosed during application

Can an Insurer Ask for More Documents Later?

Yes.

A published claim checklist is usually a starting point, not an exhaustive list.

The insurer may request further documents based on:

  • Claim type
  • Medical condition
  • Cause of loss
  • Policy wording
  • Inconsistencies in the records
  • Previous medical history
  • Legal entitlement
  • Overseas documents
  • Possible fraud
  • Need for specialist clarification

Allianz’s own claim guidance notes that listed documents serve as a guide and that further information may be requested when necessary.

A request for additional documents does not automatically mean the claim will be declined.

It means that the insurer requires more information before completing its assessment.

How to Organise Your Claim Submission

A well-organised submission can make the claim easier to review.

Arrange the documents in this order:

  1. Claim form
  2. Document checklist
  3. NRIC or passport
  4. Policy details
  5. Medical report
  6. Discharge summary
  7. Diagnostic reports
  8. Itemised bills
  9. Official receipts
  10. Additional supporting documents
  11. E-payment form
  12. Bank account evidence

Use clear file names for digital submissions, such as:

  • Claim_Form.pdf
  • NRIC_Claimant.pdf
  • Medical_Report.pdf
  • Discharge_Summary.pdf
  • Itemised_Hospital_Bill.pdf
  • Official_Receipt.pdf
  • CT_Scan_Report.pdf
  • Bank_Account_Details.pdf

Avoid submitting dozens of images with unclear names such as:

  • IMG_1024.jpg
  • Photo1.jpg
  • Scan0003.pdf

Should You Submit Everything at Once?

Where possible, submit a complete claim package.

Submitting documents one at a time can make it harder to determine whether the claim is complete.

Before submission, ask:

  • Is the correct claim form used?
  • Has every section been completed?
  • Has the claimant signed?
  • Has the doctor completed the medical report?
  • Are the bills itemised?
  • Are the receipts included?
  • Are investigation results attached?
  • Are identification documents clear?
  • Are bank details correct?
  • Are original documents required?
  • Has a copy been kept?

LIAM’s consumer education material advises policyholders to submit claim documents together with original bills and receipts, full doctors’ reports, treatment cost information and relevant referral documentation.

How Long Should You Keep Your Claim Documents?

Keep copies until:

  • The claim is fully assessed
  • Payment has been received
  • Any appeal has been completed
  • Any dispute has been resolved
  • You no longer need the records for tax, legal or medical purposes

It is sensible to keep important medical records for the long term because they may be relevant to:

  • Future insurance applications
  • Future claims
  • Medical follow-up
  • Appeals
  • Policy reviews
  • Treatment by another doctor

What Should You Do If a Hospital Will Not Release a Medical Report?

Hospitals may require:

  • Patient’s written consent
  • Medical report application form
  • Processing fee
  • Copy of identification
  • Waiting period for preparation
  • Authorisation from the patient’s representative

Ask the hospital’s medical records department about its procedure.

If the insurer has requested a particular form, provide the form to the treating doctor rather than requesting only a general hospital report.

Can Your Insurance Agent Help?

A servicing agent may assist by:

  • Identifying the relevant claim form
  • Providing the insurer’s checklist
  • Checking whether obvious documents are missing
  • Submitting documents
  • Following up on claim status
  • Explaining requests for additional information
  • Helping organise an appeal

However, the agent does not:

  • Diagnose the medical condition
  • Complete the doctor’s report
  • Decide whether the claim is covered
  • Approve or reject the claim
  • Override the policy terms
  • Guarantee payment

The final claim decision is made by the insurer based on the policy and supporting evidence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I submit a claim without the original receipt?

Possibly, depending on the insurer and circumstances.

You may need to obtain a replacement or certified copy from the hospital and explain why the original is unavailable.

Acceptance is not guaranteed.

Can I use a credit-card statement as proof of payment?

It may support the claim, but it may not replace the hospital’s official receipt and itemised bill.

The statement shows that a transaction occurred, but not necessarily what medical treatment was paid for.

Must the doctor complete the insurer’s own form?

Often, yes.

A general hospital report may not answer all the questions required for assessment.

Check with the insurer before paying for a medical report.

Who pays the medical report fee?

This depends on the insurer and policy.

In some cases, the claimant must pay the hospital or doctor for preparing the report.

The fee may not be reimbursable.

Can I submit documents through my agent?

Many insurers allow submission through a servicing agent.

For example, Allianz states that individual life claims may be submitted through the agent or at the nearest Allianz Life branch.

Keep a complete copy and obtain confirmation that the documents were submitted.

Can I submit a claim online?

Many insurers provide digital claim submission through an application, portal or electronic form.

Available functions differ by insurer and claim type.

Some claims may still require original or certified documents.

Why does the insurer need my previous medical records?

Earlier records may help determine:

  • When symptoms began
  • Whether the condition existed before coverage
  • Whether the current condition is related to earlier treatment
  • Whether the diagnosis meets the policy definition
  • Whether the application information was accurate

What if my documents are in another language?

The insurer may request a certified translation into English or Bahasa Malaysia.

This is especially common for overseas medical and death claims.

Can a claim be rejected because documents were submitted late?

Policies may contain claim-notification or document-submission requirements.

Submit the claim as soon as reasonably possible.

Where there is a delay, provide an explanation and any supporting evidence. The outcome depends on the policy terms and circumstances.

Does submitting all documents guarantee approval?

No.

Complete documents allow the insurer to assess the claim, but approval still depends on:

  • Policy coverage
  • Waiting periods
  • Exclusions
  • Medical definitions
  • Policy status
  • Benefit limits
  • Medical necessity
  • Accuracy of the information provided

Final Claim Document Checklist

Before submitting your claim, confirm that you have:

  • Used the correct claim form
  • Completed every relevant section
  • Signed all declarations and consent forms
  • Included clear identification documents
  • Provided the correct policy number
  • Asked the treating doctor to complete the medical report
  • Included the discharge summary
  • Attached all relevant diagnostic reports
  • Obtained an itemised bill
  • Included the official receipt and proof of payment
  • Added police or accident reports where applicable
  • Included overseas travel documents where relevant
  • Completed the e-payment form
  • Verified the bank account details
  • Kept copies of every document
  • Recorded the submission date and reference number

Final Thoughts

A successful insurance claim begins with complete, accurate and well-organised documentation.

The exact checklist depends on the type of claim, but the insurer will generally need evidence confirming:

  • The identity of the insured person and claimant
  • The event or medical diagnosis
  • The treatment received
  • The amount paid
  • The policy benefit being claimed
  • The person legally entitled to receive payment

For medical reimbursement claims, retain original bills, receipts, medical reports and diagnostic results.

For critical illness claims, obtain the specialist’s report and objective evidence supporting the diagnosis.

For accident and disability claims, provide clear information about the event, injury and effect on the insured person.

For death claims, prepare the death certificate, claimant’s identification, proof of relationship and any legal or investigative documents required.

Most importantly, do not wait until the final day to begin collecting documents. Hospitals, doctors, government departments and legal representatives may need time to prepare them.
