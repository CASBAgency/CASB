What Is a Reasonable and Customary Charge? Why Your Medical Card May Not Pay the Full Bill

By CASB Advisory Team · August 3, 2026 · 8 min read
Key distinction: A hospital can charge an amount, while a medical plan may assess how much of that charge is payable under its contract. The two amounts are not automatically identical.

What Does “Reasonable and Customary” Mean?

Medical policies commonly use wording such as reasonable and customary charges to assess eligible expenses. The exact definition belongs to the individual policy contract, but it generally refers to charges consistent with the usual level for comparable treatment, services or supplies under relevant circumstances.

It is not simply whatever amount appears on a bill, nor does it mean the cheapest available treatment.

Is There One Official Price List?

Usually, no single public nationwide figure applies to every doctor, hospital and clinical situation. The assessment can depend on the service, complexity, provider arrangement, location, prevailing charges and the policy wording.

Published professional-fee rules or hospital arrangements may provide context, but they do not turn every treatment into one universal retail price.

Why Might the Approved Amount Be Lower?

  • The billed amount exceeds the plan's assessed reasonable and customary level
  • A benefit has a specific schedule or sub-limit
  • Part of the service is excluded or not medically necessary under the policy definition
  • The hospital or doctor is outside an applicable panel arrangement
  • The room, implant, medicine or procedure exceeds the plan's entitlement
  • A deductible, co-payment or co-insurance applies

These reasons are different and should not be combined into one vague explanation.

Excess Charge Versus Non-Covered Item

ReasonWhat it means
Reasonable-and-customary adjustmentThe service may be eligible, but the payable amount is assessed below the billed amount.
Benefit limitThe contract caps payment for that benefit or period.
ExclusionThe contract does not cover that condition, service or circumstance.
Cost-sharingThe contract deliberately leaves part of an eligible expense to the policyholder.

Does a High Annual Limit Prevent a Shortfall?

No. An annual limit is the maximum aggregate benefit available, not a promise that every individual charge is payable in full. Other conditions, limits and assessments continue to apply even when substantial annual-limit capacity remains.

Does a Guarantee Letter Cover Everything?

No. A guarantee letter normally supports cashless admission for an eligible case based on information available at that time. Final payment remains subject to the completed medical report, itemised bill and policy terms.

Practical point: Ask whether a hospital deposit or discharge balance relates to pending assessment, policy cost-sharing, a non-covered item or an amount above the approved charge.

A Simple Illustration

Suppose a hospital bills RM10,000. This does not automatically mean the plan pays RM10,000. If an item is excluded, a deductible applies or part of a professional fee is assessed above the payable level, the approved claim can be lower.

This illustration is not a benchmark or actual claim outcome. Every assessment depends on the policy and case documents.

What Written Explanation Should You Request?

  • The exact bill item being adjusted
  • The amount billed, amount approved and difference
  • The policy clause, benefit schedule or assessment basis relied upon
  • Whether further clinical information could affect the decision
  • The insurer's review or appeal procedure
  • Whether the hospital can clarify or correct the itemised bill

What Should You Do If You Disagree?

  1. Compare the final itemised bill with the insurer's settlement statement.
  2. Ask for a written reason rather than relying on a verbal summary.
  3. Check the policy wording that applied when treatment occurred.
  4. Request review with supporting medical or billing documents.
  5. Use the insurer's complaint process and, when appropriate, the relevant independent dispute-resolution or regulatory channel.

How to Reduce Surprises

Before planned treatment, request an estimate, identify all attending specialists, confirm panel status and ask the insurer about limits, cost-sharing and pre-authorisation. During admission, request an updated estimate if the treatment plan changes.

Neither a hospital estimate nor an early guarantee letter can eliminate every uncertainty, but written information makes later differences easier to understand and review.
