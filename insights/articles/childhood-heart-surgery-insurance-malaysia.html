Can You Buy Insurance After Childhood Heart Surgery? What Malaysian Underwriters May Review

By CASB Advisory Team · August 3, 2026 · 8 min read

The Short Answer: You Can Apply

Having heart surgery as a baby or child does not automatically mean a person can never obtain insurance. An application may still be considered, but acceptance and terms depend on individual underwriting.

The insurer will assess what the original condition was, whether it was fully corrected, the person's current heart function and the type of cover requested. A medical card, critical-illness benefit and life cover can produce different decisions from the same history.

Important distinction: being able to submit an application is not the same as being guaranteed standard acceptance.

What Type of Childhood Heart Condition Was It?

“Heart surgery” is not a diagnosis. The underwriting picture can differ greatly between a repaired structural defect and a condition that still affects rhythm, valves, pressure or heart function.

Relevant diagnoses may include atrial or ventricular septal defects, patent ductus arteriosus, valve conditions, Tetralogy of Fallot or other congenital abnormalities. The name of the operation, age at surgery and whether further procedures were required all matter.

An Allianz Malaysia child-related medical-report form illustrates the level of clinical detail that heart conditions can require: it asks about specific congenital diagnoses, anatomical findings and whether surgical closure is warranted. This is a claim form, not an underwriting promise, but it shows why “I had surgery when I was young” is usually not enough information.

What Underwriters May Review Today

  • The exact diagnosis and date it was first identified
  • The operation performed and any complications
  • Whether the repair was complete or residual defects remain
  • Current echocardiogram and electrocardiogram findings
  • Heart rhythm, chamber size, valve function and pressures
  • Medication, specialist follow-up and future treatment plans
  • Symptoms such as breathlessness, chest pain, fainting or reduced exercise capacity
  • Later admissions, procedures or related conditions

The longer a person has remained well can be helpful context, but time alone does not replace current medical evidence.

Possible Underwriting Outcomes

Possible outcomeWhat it may mean
Standard termsAccepted without special pricing or exclusions
Premium loadingCover offered at a higher premium
Specific exclusionCertain heart-related conditions or treatment excluded
DefermentDecision postponed for updated tests or a period of stability
Modified benefitsOne type or amount of cover accepted while another is not
DeclineThe insurer does not offer the requested cover at that time

These are general possibilities, not predictions. Some products may already exclude congenital conditions under their wording, while individually underwritten cover may be offered only on terms confirmed in writing.

Medical, Critical Illness and Life Cover Are Different

A medical policy considers future hospital and treatment costs. Critical-illness cover depends on defined serious events and benefit wording. Life insurance assesses mortality risk. Because the insured events differ, one application may be accepted while another is loaded, excluded, deferred or declined.

Do not assume that an unsuccessful medical-card application means life cover is impossible—or that life-cover acceptance means future heart treatment is covered by a medical card.

Documents to Prepare Before Applying

  1. Original diagnosis and discharge summary, if available
  2. Operation report or a specialist letter describing the procedure
  3. Most recent cardiologist report
  4. Current echocardiogram and ECG results
  5. Medication and follow-up history
  6. Records of later admissions or repeat procedures
  7. A doctor's statement on present function, symptoms and future monitoring

If childhood records no longer exist, say so honestly. Ask the current cardiologist what can be confirmed from examination and available history instead of guessing dates or diagnoses.

Full Disclosure Protects the Application

Do not omit the surgery because it happened long ago, because there are no current symptoms or because a family member completed the form. Allianz Malaysia's duty-of-disclosure notice says consumer applicants must take reasonable care to answer proposal questions fully and accurately. Inaccurate answers can affect the policy and future claims under the remedies allowed by law.

Review the completed proposal before signing. Correct unclear answers in writing, keep a copy and retain every medical document submitted.

How an Adviser Can Help—Without Deciding the Outcome

An adviser can help identify the relevant application questions, organise records, request a preliminary review where available and explain the written terms offered. The adviser cannot diagnose the condition, alter medical facts or guarantee an underwriting decision.

If an offer includes loading, exclusion or modified benefits, compare the actual written terms—not only the monthly premium. Understand what is covered, what is excluded and whether another form of protection should also be considered.

Official Sources

This guide was checked against Allianz Malaysia's official duty-of-disclosure notice, its explanation of application and underwriting support, and official Allianz medical documents illustrating the history and clinical detail requested for child-related heart conditions. Those forms do not state who will be accepted; individual underwriting remains necessary.
