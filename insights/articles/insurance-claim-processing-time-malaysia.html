How Long Does an Insurance Claim Take in Malaysia?

By CASB Advisory Team · July 22, 2026 · 28 min read

Introduction

After submitting an insurance claim, one of the first questions most policyholders ask is:

“How long will it take before I receive the claim payment?”

There is no single processing period that applies to every insurance claim.

A straightforward medical reimbursement claim with complete documents may be assessed relatively quickly. A death, critical illness or disability claim involving incomplete records, overseas documents or a complex medical history may take considerably longer.

The processing time generally depends on:

  • The type of claim
  • Whether all required documents have been submitted
  • The complexity of the medical condition or event
  • Whether additional medical records are required
  • Whether the insurer needs to investigate the claim
  • Whether the policy coverage is clear
  • How quickly hospitals, doctors and other parties respond
  • Whether the insurer has approved the claim but is still arranging payment

This guide explains how insurance claim processing works, why some claims take longer and what you can do to avoid unnecessary delays.

Is There a Standard Insurance Claim Processing Time?

Not one that applies to every insurer and every claim.

Insurers may publish service timelines for particular claim categories, but those timelines usually begin only after the insurer has received all necessary information and documents.

For example, Allianz Malaysia states that for individual life claims, it processes the claim within seven working days after receiving all necessary documents. Once the claim is approved, payment is issued within a further seven working days.

This should not be interpreted as a guarantee that every claim will be paid within 14 working days from the date the claimant first contacts the insurer.

The insurer’s processing period may not begin while it is still waiting for:

  • A completed claim form
  • A medical report
  • Original hospital bills
  • Diagnostic results
  • Previous medical records
  • A police report
  • Legal documents
  • Proof of identity
  • Correct bank details
  • Clarification from the claimant

The most important distinction is between:

  1. The date the claim is first notified
  2. The date the complete claim documents are received
  3. The date the claim is approved
  4. The date the payment reaches the claimant

These are not necessarily the same date.

The Four Main Stages of an Insurance Claim

Stage 1: Claim Notification

This is when the insurer is first informed that a claim may be made.

Notification may be submitted through:

  • The insurer’s mobile application
  • Online portal
  • Servicing agent
  • Branch office
  • Customer service department
  • Email
  • Hospital
  • Employer’s human resources department

At this stage, the insurer may create a claim record and issue a reference number.

Claim notification alone does not necessarily mean that the claim is ready for assessment.

The insurer may still need the completed forms and supporting documents.

Stage 2: Document Collection

This is often the longest part of the process.

The claimant may need to obtain documents from:

  • Hospital
  • Treating doctor
  • Specialist
  • Clinic
  • Police
  • Employer
  • Government department
  • Bank
  • Foreign authority
  • Court or legal representative

Some documents can be obtained immediately, while others may take days or weeks to prepare.

For example, a hospital may need time to produce:

  • A formal medical report
  • Specialist statement
  • Discharge summary
  • Itemised bill
  • Histopathology report
  • Surgical notes
  • Previous admission records

Until the required documents are received, the claim may remain incomplete.

Stage 3: Claim Assessment

Once the necessary documents are available, the insurer assesses the claim.

The insurer may review:

  • Policy status
  • Benefit amount
  • Waiting periods
  • Exclusions
  • Diagnosis
  • Medical necessity
  • Date symptoms began
  • Previous medical history
  • Cause of accident or death
  • Identity of the claimant
  • Legal entitlement to payment
  • Amount of eligible expenses

The insurer may approve the claim, partially approve it, request further information or decline it.

Stage 4: Payment

After approval, the insurer arranges payment.

Payment may be made through:

  • Direct credit
  • Electronic transfer
  • Cheque
  • Payment to the hospital
  • Payment to a nominee or legal representative
  • Payment to an employer under group coverage

Even after payment is issued, it may take additional time to appear in the recipient’s bank account.

Incorrect or incomplete bank information can delay this stage.

How Long Does a Medical Reimbursement Claim Take?

A straightforward medical reimbursement claim may be processed relatively quickly once the insurer receives a complete submission.

Documents commonly required include:

  • Claim form
  • Medical report
  • Discharge summary
  • Original itemised bill
  • Official receipt
  • Diagnostic reports
  • Proof of payment
  • Identification documents
  • Bank account details

The claim may take longer when:

  • The diagnosis is unclear
  • The admission occurred shortly after the policy started
  • Previous medical records are required
  • The insurer is investigating a possible pre-existing condition
  • The hospital bill is incomplete
  • The treatment occurred overseas
  • The admission may not have been medically necessary
  • The claim involves an exclusion
  • The insurer has not received the original documents

Allianz Malaysia’s published timeline for individual claims begins after all necessary documents have been received: processing within seven working days, followed by payment issuance within seven working days after approval.

Actual completion time may be longer where the claim requires further information.

How Long Does a Cashless Hospital Claim Take?

Cashless claims work differently from reimbursement claims.

During admission, the hospital sends a Guarantee Letter request to the insurer or its third-party administrator.

There may be two separate GL stages:

  1. Initial GL for admission
  2. Final GL for discharge

The initial request is based on preliminary information such as:

  • Symptoms
  • Initial diagnosis
  • Proposed treatment
  • Estimated bill
  • Doctor’s admission notes

The final GL is based on:

  • Final diagnosis
  • Actual treatment
  • Itemised hospital bill
  • Medication
  • Procedures performed
  • Discharge information

A straightforward GL may be processed quickly, but it may take longer when the insurer requires:

  • Additional medical information
  • Previous medical records
  • Investigation results
  • Clarification from the attending doctor
  • Confirmation of policy benefits
  • Explanation of medical necessity

A pending GL does not automatically mean the claim has been rejected.

It may simply mean that the insurer does not yet have enough information to make a decision.

Why Does Hospital Discharge Sometimes Take So Long?

A patient may be medically ready to leave the hospital but still need to wait for billing and insurance clearance.

Before discharge can be completed, the hospital may need to:

  1. Receive the doctor’s discharge instruction.
  2. Finalise all treatment charges.
  3. Prepare the itemised bill.
  4. Submit the final GL request.
  5. Respond to any insurer questions.
  6. Receive the final GL.
  7. Calculate the patient’s outstanding amount.
  8. Collect payment for non-covered charges.

The delay may be caused by the hospital, doctor, insurer or a combination of these parties.

The final GL process can take longer when:

  • The doctor has not completed the discharge documents
  • The bill is still being updated
  • The insurer has queries about specific charges
  • The treatment differs from the initial admission plan
  • The final diagnosis has changed
  • The policyholder upgraded the hospital room
  • A deductible or co-payment must be calculated
  • Certain expenses are not covered

Patients should avoid arranging transport immediately after the doctor announces discharge, as administrative clearance may still be required.

How Long Does a Critical Illness Claim Take?

A critical illness claim generally requires more detailed medical assessment than a routine hospital reimbursement claim.

The insurer must confirm that the diagnosis satisfies the precise definition stated in the policy.

It may need documents such as:

  • Specialist’s medical report
  • Histopathology report
  • Biopsy result
  • CT or MRI report
  • Cardiac investigations
  • Surgical report
  • Laboratory results
  • Discharge summary
  • Previous medical records

A diagnosis with the same name as a covered critical illness does not automatically mean the policy definition has been met.

For example, the insurer may need to establish:

  • Cancer type and stage
  • Whether a tumour is invasive
  • Severity of a heart condition
  • Evidence of permanent neurological damage
  • Degree of kidney failure
  • Duration of disability
  • Whether the condition meets an early-stage or advanced-stage definition

The claim may take longer if the diagnosis is recent and the required medical evidence is not yet available.

How Long Does a Life Insurance Death Claim Take?

A straightforward death claim with a valid nomination and complete documents may be assessed more quickly than a case involving legal or investigative complications.

Common documents include:

  • Death claim form
  • Death certificate
  • Claimant’s identification
  • Deceased person’s identification
  • Policy information
  • Proof of relationship
  • Medical report
  • Bank details

The process may take longer where:

  • There is no valid nominee
  • Legal authority must be established
  • Probate or letters of administration are required
  • The death occurred overseas
  • The cause of death is unclear
  • A post-mortem report is pending
  • A police investigation is ongoing
  • The death occurred shortly after the policy began
  • The insurer needs previous medical records
  • The death was accidental or suspicious
  • Several parties claim entitlement to the proceeds

A death claim may therefore take anywhere from a relatively short period for a straightforward case to several months for a legally or medically complex claim.

How Long Does an Accidental Death Claim Take?

Accidental death claims usually require additional evidence concerning the circumstances of death.

The insurer may request:

  • Police report
  • Post-mortem report
  • Toxicology report
  • Coroner’s findings
  • Inquest report
  • Accident investigation report
  • Witness statements
  • Driving licence
  • Newspaper report
  • Photographs
  • Hospital records

Some of these documents are controlled by government authorities and may not be immediately available.

The insurer may be unable to complete the claim until the official cause and circumstances of death have been established.

How Long Does a Disability Claim Take?

Disability claims can take longer than many other claim types because disability may need to be assessed over time.

Depending on the policy, the insurer may need to determine whether the insured person is:

  • Totally disabled
  • Permanently disabled
  • Unable to perform their occupation
  • Unable to perform any occupation
  • Unable to carry out activities of daily living
  • Disabled for the required continuous period

Documents may include:

  • Specialist’s report
  • Functional assessment
  • Rehabilitation records
  • Employer’s confirmation
  • Job description
  • Income records
  • Medical leave records
  • Diagnostic results
  • Follow-up assessment

The insurer may request a waiting or observation period before confirming that the disability is permanent.

It may also require reassessment by an independent doctor.

How Long Does an Accident Medical Claim Take?

A simple accident medical expense claim may be processed quickly if the documents clearly show:

  • When the accident happened
  • How it happened
  • What injury occurred
  • What treatment was received
  • How much was paid

It may take longer where:

  • The accident description is unclear
  • The police report is pending
  • The claim form and medical report contain different information
  • The injury may be related to an existing condition
  • The accident occurred during work
  • Another insurer or employer is involved
  • The event may fall under a policy exclusion

Consistency across the accident statement, police report and medical records is important.

How Long Does an Overseas Claim Take?

Overseas claims may take longer because the insurer may need to verify documents issued in another country.

The claimant may need to provide:

  • Overseas hospital bill
  • Official receipt
  • Medical report
  • Discharge summary
  • Passport travel records
  • Proof of payment
  • Currency conversion details
  • Certified translation
  • Police or government reports
  • Evidence that the treatment was an emergency

Delays can occur because:

  • The documents are not in English or Bahasa Malaysia
  • Certification or legalisation is required
  • The overseas hospital is slow to respond
  • Currency conversion must be verified
  • Coverage is limited to emergencies
  • The insurer must compare the charges with applicable policy limits

What Does “Complete Documents” Actually Mean?

A claim is not necessarily complete simply because the claimant has submitted several documents.

A complete submission means that the insurer has received all information reasonably required to assess that particular claim.

For a hospitalisation claim, complete documents may include:

  • Signed claim form
  • Doctor-completed medical report
  • Itemised bill
  • Official receipt
  • Discharge summary
  • Diagnostic reports
  • Bank information

For a death claim, complete documents may include:

  • Death certificate
  • Claim form
  • Identification documents
  • Proof of relationship
  • Medical or police documents
  • Legal authority
  • Bank information

The required documents may change after the insurer reviews the initial submission.

For example, the insurer may discover that:

  • Earlier symptoms require investigation
  • The hospital report is incomplete
  • The claimant’s legal status is unclear
  • A diagnostic report is missing
  • The policy application requires review
  • Conflicting dates need clarification

Allianz explains that where additional documents are required for a life claim, the claimant will be informed through a deferment letter.

What Is a Deferment Letter?

A deferment letter informs the claimant that the insurer cannot complete the assessment yet because further information is required.

It may request:

  • Medical report
  • Previous clinic records
  • Specialist’s clarification
  • Diagnostic test results
  • Police report
  • Post-mortem report
  • Legal documents
  • Proof of relationship
  • Corrected claim form
  • Bank information

A deferment is not the same as a claim rejection.

It means the decision has been postponed until the insurer receives the outstanding information.

The claimant should read the letter carefully and respond to every request.

Submitting only some of the requested documents may lead to another deferment.

When Does the Processing Clock Begin?

This is one of the biggest sources of misunderstanding.

A claimant may say:

“I submitted my claim one month ago.”

However, the insurer may say:

“The complete documents were received only last week.”

Both statements can be true.

The timeline may look like this:

  • 1 August: Claim first notified
  • 5 August: Claim form submitted
  • 12 August: Insurer requests medical report
  • 25 August: Hospital prepares medical report
  • 28 August: Medical report submitted
  • 2 September: Insurer requests earlier clinic records
  • 10 September: Earlier records received
  • 11 September: Claim becomes complete
  • 18 September: Claim approved
  • 25 September: Payment issued

Although the claim was first notified on 1 August, the insurer could not fully assess it until the necessary information was received.

That is why claimants should track both the original submission date and the date the final outstanding document was delivered.

Working Days vs Calendar Days

Insurers commonly express processing periods in working days.

Working days generally exclude:

  • Saturdays
  • Sundays
  • Public holidays

Therefore, seven working days may extend across more than one calendar week.

For example, if a complete claim is received on a Friday, the following weekend will normally not count as working days.

Public holidays can extend the overall calendar period further.

Common Reasons Insurance Claims Take Longer

1. Incomplete Claim Form

The insurer may be unable to proceed if:

  • Questions are unanswered
  • Signatures are missing
  • Consent has not been given
  • The policy number is incorrect
  • The diagnosis or event date is unclear
  • Bank information is missing

Review every page before submission.

2. Missing Medical Report

A discharge summary may not contain enough information for the insurer to assess:

  • When symptoms began
  • Whether the condition was pre-existing
  • Whether the treatment was medically necessary
  • Whether the diagnosis meets the policy definition

A doctor-completed medical report may therefore be required.

3. Hospital Takes Time to Prepare Records

Hospitals may require several working days or longer to prepare:

  • Formal medical reports
  • Complete admission records
  • Surgical notes
  • Histopathology reports
  • Previous medical files

The insurer cannot control how quickly every hospital or doctor responds.

4. Previous Medical Records Are Required

The insurer may need records from earlier:

  • Clinic consultations
  • Hospital admissions
  • Medical screenings
  • Diagnostic tests
  • Specialist appointments
  • Medication history

These records help establish whether the condition began before the policy or waiting period ended.

5. Conflicting Information

Assessment may be delayed if different documents contain inconsistent information.

Examples include different dates for:

  • First symptoms
  • First consultation
  • Accident
  • Diagnosis
  • Admission
  • Previous treatment

The insurer may request clarification from the claimant or doctor.

6. Diagnosis Is Not Final

The doctor may initially record symptoms rather than a confirmed diagnosis.

The insurer may wait for:

  • Biopsy
  • Histopathology
  • CT scan
  • MRI
  • Laboratory results
  • Specialist review

This is common in serious or complex medical conditions.

7. Possible Pre-Existing Condition

The insurer may investigate whether the condition or related symptoms existed before coverage began.

This may require:

  • Earlier medical records
  • Application form review
  • Doctor’s explanation
  • Timeline of symptoms
  • Previous test results

8. Claim Occurred Soon After Policy Commencement

A claim made shortly after a policy starts may receive additional review.

The insurer may need to confirm:

  • Whether the waiting period applies
  • When symptoms first appeared
  • Whether treatment had been planned earlier
  • Whether relevant medical information was disclosed

A claim occurring soon after policy commencement is not automatically invalid, but it may require closer assessment.

9. Police or Government Investigation Is Pending

Accident and death claims may depend on:

  • Police investigation
  • Post-mortem report
  • Coroner’s findings
  • Inquest result
  • Toxicology report
  • Government certification

These documents may take time to become available.

10. Legal Entitlement Is Unclear

A life insurance claim may be delayed where:

  • There is no nominee
  • A nominee has died
  • The nomination is disputed
  • The claimant is a minor
  • Several beneficiaries are involved
  • Probate is required
  • Letters of administration are pending
  • The estate is disputed

The insurer must ensure payment is made to the correct person.

11. Overseas Documents Require Verification

The insurer may need:

  • Certified translation
  • Confirmation from the hospital
  • Embassy verification
  • Proof of travel dates
  • Currency conversion
  • Foreign death registration

12. Potential Fraud or Misrepresentation

The insurer may conduct a more detailed investigation if:

  • Documents appear altered
  • Medical information is inconsistent
  • The claimant gives conflicting accounts
  • The event is suspicious
  • The claim amount is unusual
  • Several similar claims are identified
  • Identity or payment details cannot be verified

A legitimate claimant should cooperate and provide clear supporting evidence.

13. High or Complex Claim Amount

Large claims may require:

  • Senior review
  • Specialist medical opinion
  • Legal review
  • Reinsurance notification
  • Additional internal approval

This does not automatically mean the claim is in doubt.

It may simply require a more detailed assessment process.

14. Incorrect Bank Details

A claim may already be approved but payment may fail because:

  • Account number is incorrect
  • Account has been closed
  • Account holder’s name does not match
  • Bank document is unclear
  • Joint-account information is incomplete
  • The claimant is not the account holder

Check the details carefully before submission.

Does a Delay Mean the Claim Will Be Rejected?

No.

A delayed claim may eventually be:

  • Fully approved
  • Partially approved
  • Approved after additional documents
  • Declined
  • Withdrawn by the claimant

A delay often means that the insurer is waiting for information.

However, claimants should not ignore a prolonged delay.

They should ask:

  • What is currently outstanding?
  • Who is responsible for providing it?
  • When was it requested?
  • Has the insurer received it?
  • Is anything else required?
  • Is the claim now considered complete?
  • What is the next assessment step?

How Can You Speed Up an Insurance Claim?

You cannot control every part of the process, but you can reduce avoidable delays.

Submit the Correct Claim Form

Different benefits may require different forms.

Do not use a general medical form for a critical illness, disability or death claim unless instructed.

Submit All Available Documents Together

Where possible, submit a complete package containing:

  • Claim form
  • Identification
  • Medical report
  • Diagnostic evidence
  • Bills
  • Receipts
  • Bank details
  • Supporting records

Submitting documents separately can make it harder to confirm whether the claim is complete.

Ask the Doctor to Be Specific

The medical report should clearly state:

  • Diagnosis
  • Date symptoms began
  • First consultation date
  • Investigation findings
  • Treatment
  • Relevant medical history
  • Medical necessity

Vague medical reports often lead to further questions.

Check the Documents for Consistency

Compare:

  • Claim form
  • Medical report
  • Discharge summary
  • Police report
  • Bills
  • Accident statement

Correct obvious factual errors before submission.

Do not alter a doctor’s report yourself. Ask the hospital or doctor to correct it properly.

Use Clear Digital Copies

For online claims:

  • Scan every page clearly
  • Do not crop important information
  • Use readable file names
  • Combine related pages
  • Avoid blurry photographs
  • Ensure signatures are visible

Provide Previous Medical Records Promptly

Where earlier records are requested, contact the relevant clinics or hospitals immediately.

Do not assume the insurer will automatically obtain every record.

A signed medical authorisation may still be required.

Track Every Submission

Record:

  • Date of claim notification
  • Date documents were submitted
  • Method of submission
  • Claim reference number
  • Documents included
  • Additional requests
  • Date additional documents were sent
  • Name of representative contacted
  • Latest claim status

Keep email confirmations, delivery receipts and screenshots.

Respond to Deferment Requests Completely

If the insurer asks for five items, respond to all five.

Where an item is unavailable, explain:

  • Why it is unavailable
  • What attempts were made to obtain it
  • Whether an alternative document exists
  • When it may become available

Do not simply ignore the missing item.

Verify Bank Details Early

Submit:

  • Correct account number
  • Correct account holder’s name
  • Clear bank statement or passbook page
  • Required direct-credit form

How Often Should You Follow Up?

Following up every hour is unlikely to speed up the claim.

A more effective approach is to follow up at meaningful stages:

  • After initial submission, confirm receipt.
  • After submitting additional documents, confirm they were received.
  • Ask whether the claim is now complete.
  • Follow up after the insurer’s stated processing period has passed.
  • Request a written status if the claim remains pending.

Avoid relying solely on verbal updates.

Written records are useful if the claim later requires escalation.

What Questions Should You Ask When Following Up?

Instead of asking only, “Is my claim approved?”, ask:

  1. Has my claim been registered?
  2. What is the claim reference number?
  3. Which documents have been received?
  4. Is anything still outstanding?
  5. Is the claim considered complete?
  6. Is the claim under medical, legal or administrative review?
  7. Has information been requested from a hospital or doctor?
  8. When was that request sent?
  9. Has the requested information been received?
  10. What is the next step?
  11. What processing timeframe applies after completion?
  12. Will I receive a written decision?

These questions help identify where the delay is occurring.

How Can You Check an Allianz Claim Status?

Allianz Malaysia states that individual claim information can be checked through MyAllianz, with claim information updated after 24 hours. Policyholders may also contact their servicing agent or Allianz’s contact centre.

When checking the status, prepare:

  • Policy number
  • Claim reference number
  • Insured person’s name
  • NRIC or passport number
  • Date of submission

Do not share confidential claim information through unofficial communication channels.

What Should You Do If the Claim Is Taking Too Long?

Step 1: Obtain a Clear Status

Ask the insurer to confirm in writing:

  • Whether the claim is complete
  • What remains outstanding
  • Why the information is required
  • Whether the documents have been requested
  • What stage the claim is at

Step 2: Contact the Responsible Party

If the insurer is waiting for a medical report, contact the hospital.

If it is waiting for a police report, follow up with the investigating authority.

If legal documents are required, speak to the relevant legal representative.

Step 3: Escalate Within the Insurer

If the claim has exceeded the insurer’s stated timeframe without a satisfactory explanation, request escalation to:

  • Claims supervisor
  • Customer service manager
  • Complaints unit
  • Branch manager

Provide the claim reference and a clear timeline of events.

Step 4: Submit a Formal Complaint

A formal complaint should include:

  • Policyholder’s name
  • Policy number
  • Claim number
  • Date of claim notification
  • Date complete documents were submitted
  • Chronology of follow-ups
  • Outstanding issue
  • Supporting correspondence
  • Resolution requested

Keep the complaint factual and organised.

Step 5: Consider External Dispute Resolution

Where the matter remains unresolved, policyholders may consider the complaint and dispute-resolution avenues available in Malaysia.

The appropriate avenue depends on:

  • Type of complaint
  • Amount involved
  • Whether the insurer has issued a final decision
  • Whether the matter concerns claim delay, rejection or interpretation
  • Applicable eligibility requirements

Before escalating externally, obtain the insurer’s written response and keep all supporting records.

Does the Insurer Pay Interest for a Delayed Claim?

This depends on:

  • Policy terms
  • Applicable law or regulatory requirements
  • Type of claim
  • Reason for the delay
  • Whether the insurer had all necessary information
  • Whether payment was unreasonably withheld

A claimant should not assume that every delayed claim automatically attracts interest or compensation.

Where a substantial delay has occurred, request a written explanation and ask what remedy is available.

Can an Agent Guarantee a Claim Timeline?

No.

A servicing agent may help by:

  • Providing claim forms
  • Checking documents
  • Submitting the claim
  • Following up with the insurer
  • Explaining outstanding requirements
  • Helping prepare an appeal

However, the agent cannot control:

  • Hospital processing times
  • Medical report preparation
  • Police investigations
  • Legal proceedings
  • Insurer assessment
  • Final approval
  • Bank processing

An agent should not promise that a claim will definitely be approved or paid by a particular date unless the insurer has formally confirmed it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a medical insurance claim usually take?

It depends on whether the claim is cashless or reimbursement-based, whether all documents have been received and whether further medical review is required.

Some insurers publish processing timelines that begin after complete documents are received.

Does the timeline begin when I submit the first form?

Not necessarily.

The insurer may only treat the claim as complete after receiving all required supporting documents.

Why has the insurer asked for more documents?

The insurer may need further evidence to verify the diagnosis, medical history, accident, policy coverage or claimant’s legal entitlement.

This does not automatically mean the claim will be rejected.

What is the difference between processing and payment time?

Processing time refers to the period required to assess and decide the claim.

Payment time refers to the period required to issue payment after approval.

Can a straightforward claim still be delayed?

Yes.

Possible reasons include missing signatures, unclear bank details, hospital delays or incomplete medical reports.

Why does a critical illness claim take longer than a hospital claim?

The insurer must determine whether the diagnosis meets the precise critical illness definition in the policy.

This often requires specialist and diagnostic evidence.

Why does an accidental death claim take longer?

The insurer may need official police, post-mortem, toxicology or investigation reports before confirming the cause and circumstances of death.

Can I complain if the claim takes too long?

Yes.

Start by requesting a written status and escalating through the insurer’s formal complaints process.

Keep all records of documents and follow-ups.

Does following up every day speed up the claim?

Not necessarily.

It is more useful to identify what is outstanding and confirm when it has been received.

Can I submit additional documents through my agent?

Usually, an agent can assist with submission and follow-up, subject to the insurer’s process.

Keep your own complete copy and proof of submission.

Does a pending claim mean it has been rejected?

No.

Pending means that the claim is still under assessment or awaiting information.

A claim is not rejected until the insurer issues a decision.

A Practical Claim Timeline Example

A relatively straightforward medical reimbursement claim might follow this process:

Day 1

The policyholder submits:

  • Claim form
  • Medical report
  • Discharge summary
  • Bills
  • Receipts
  • Bank details

Day 3

The insurer acknowledges receipt and registers the claim.

Day 5

The insurer identifies that one diagnostic report is missing.

Day 8

The claimant submits the missing report.

Day 9

The insurer confirms that the claim documents are complete.

Following Working Days

The insurer assesses:

  • Coverage
  • Diagnosis
  • Medical necessity
  • Policy limits
  • Eligible expenses

After Approval

Payment is prepared and issued to the claimant’s bank account.

The exact timing varies by insurer and claim circumstances.

A More Complex Claim Timeline Example

A critical illness claim may follow this process:

Week 1

The claimant submits the claim form, specialist report and discharge summary.

Week 2

The insurer requests the histopathology report and earlier medical records.

Week 3

The hospital provides the histopathology report.

Week 5

The earlier clinic provides previous records.

Week 6

The insurer’s medical team reviews whether the diagnosis meets the policy definition.

Week 7

The insurer requests clarification from the specialist.

Week 8

The specialist replies.

Week 9

The insurer completes assessment and issues its decision.

The delay in this example is not necessarily caused by the insurer alone. Much of the time is spent obtaining medical evidence from external parties.

Claim Processing Checklist

Before asking how long the claim will take, confirm:

  • Has the claim been registered?
  • Do I have a claim reference number?
  • Has the insurer received the claim form?
  • Has the medical report been submitted?
  • Are all bills and receipts included?
  • Have diagnostic reports been submitted?
  • Are identification documents complete?
  • Are bank details correct?
  • Has the insurer requested further information?
  • Has that information been provided?
  • Is the claim now considered complete?
  • What processing timeframe applies?
  • Has the claim been approved?
  • Has payment been issued?

Final Thoughts

There is no universal answer to how long an insurance claim takes.

The timeline depends on the claim type, complexity and completeness of the supporting evidence.

A straightforward claim may be assessed within an insurer’s published service timeline once all necessary documents are available.

A complex claim may take longer because the insurer needs:

  • Additional medical evidence
  • Previous medical records
  • Police or government reports
  • Legal documents
  • Specialist clarification
  • Overseas verification
  • Further investigation

The most effective way to reduce delays is to submit a complete, accurate and well-organised claim package from the beginning.

Remember:

  • Claim notification is not the same as complete submission.
  • A request for additional documents is not a rejection.
  • Processing time and payment time are separate.
  • Working days do not include weekends and public holidays.
  • Keep records of every submission and follow-up.
  • Ask whether the claim is officially considered complete.
  • Request a written explanation when a claim remains pending beyond the stated timeframe.

A claim is easier to track when you know exactly what has been submitted, what remains outstanding and which party is responsible for the next step.
