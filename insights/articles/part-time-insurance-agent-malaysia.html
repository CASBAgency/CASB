Part-Time Insurance Agent in Malaysia: What You Need to Know Before You Start

By CASB Advisory Team · July 8, 2026 · 6 min read
"Can I become an insurance agent part-time?"

It is one of the most common questions asked by people who are curious about the industry.

Maybe you already have a full-time job. Maybe you are looking for an additional source of income. Or perhaps you are interested in the insurance profession but are not ready to leave the stability of your current career.

The good news?

Yes, many people begin their insurance journey on a part-time basis.

However, "part-time" does not necessarily mean "easy."

Before you decide whether it is right for you, here are a few important things you should know.

Why More Malaysians Are Considering It

Living costs continue to rise, while salaries do not always keep pace.

As a result, many professionals are looking for ways to diversify their income instead of relying solely on a monthly paycheck.

An insurance career offers something many side hustles do not. It allows you to build long-term relationships, develop professional skills, and potentially earn recurring income rather than being paid only for hours worked.

For some, it eventually becomes a full-time career. For others, it remains a rewarding part-time profession that complements their primary job.

Can You Really Do It Part-Time?

Yes, but it depends on your expectations.

Many successful advisers started by meeting clients after office hours, on weekends, or through personal referrals.

Unlike shift-based part-time jobs, insurance gives you greater flexibility over when you meet clients. However, flexibility does not eliminate the work.

You will still need time for:

  • Meeting prospective clients
  • Learning products and regulations
  • Attending training sessions
  • Following up with enquiries
  • Handling documentation
  • Providing after-sales service

The number of hours may be part-time. The level of professionalism should never be.

It Is More Than Selling Policies

One misconception is that insurance agents spend their day persuading people to buy products.

In reality, much of the work involves helping clients understand risks they may not have considered.

You might discuss:

  • Medical expenses
  • Income protection
  • Critical illness planning
  • Family financial security
  • Existing insurance coverage
  • Long-term financial goals

The best conversations are not about selling. They are about helping people make informed decisions.

You Need to Be Willing to Learn

Insurance is a regulated profession. Before you begin advising clients, you will need to complete the required licensing and training.

Even after obtaining your licence, learning does not stop.

Products evolve. Medical costs change. Regulations are updated.

Successful advisers invest continuously in improving both their technical knowledge and communication skills.

Your Existing Career Can Be an Advantage

Many people assume they need a background in finance to succeed. That is rarely the case.

Teachers, engineers, healthcare professionals, accountants, lawyers, entrepreneurs, and IT specialists often bring valuable experience into the industry.

Their existing professions help them understand clients' concerns, build credibility, and develop strong networks.

In many cases, your current career is not an obstacle. It becomes one of your greatest strengths.

Can You Earn Good Income Part-Time?

Potentially, yes.

Unlike traditional part-time jobs that pay by the hour, insurance income is generally performance-based.

That means your earnings depend on the value you provide, the relationships you build, and the consistency of your efforts.

Some advisers earn enough to cover monthly commitments. Others eventually replace their full-time income.

Of course, there are also people who earn very little because they do not commit enough time or effort.

There are no guaranteed results, but there is significant upside for those who treat it professionally.

Balancing Two Careers Is Not Always Easy

Working a full-time job while building an insurance business requires discipline.

You may find yourself:

  • Meeting clients after work.
  • Spending weekends on appointments.
  • Studying at night.
  • Responding to enquiries outside office hours.

The first few months often require sacrifices.

The key is treating your part-time career with the same professionalism as your full-time one.

Be Aware of Your Employment Contract

Before starting any side business, it is important to review your employment agreement.

Some employers allow outside work without restrictions. Others require disclosure or approval.

Certain industries may also have conflict-of-interest policies that limit external business activities.

Understanding these obligations early can help you avoid unnecessary complications later.

Technology Has Made It Easier Than Ever

Today's advisers have access to tools that did not exist a decade ago.

Video calls, digital applications, electronic signatures, CRM systems, AI assistants, and automated follow-ups have made it much easier to manage clients while balancing another career.

Technology will not replace the human relationship at the heart of financial advice, but it can significantly improve efficiency and reduce administrative work.

Is It Right for You?

A part-time insurance career is not for everyone.

If you are looking for guaranteed income with minimal effort, you will probably be disappointed.

But if you are willing to learn, communicate with people, and consistently dedicate time each week, it can become more than just a side income.

It can be an opportunity to build new skills, expand your professional network, and create an additional source of long-term financial growth.

Final Thoughts

Starting as a part-time insurance agent allows you to explore the profession without immediately giving up your existing career.

It gives you the opportunity to learn the business, gain practical experience, and decide whether it is something you truly enjoy.

Success does not depend on whether you start part-time or full-time.

It depends on your willingness to learn, your commitment to serving clients well, and your consistency over time.

For many advisers, the journey begins with just a few evenings and weekends.

Where it leads after that is entirely up to them.
